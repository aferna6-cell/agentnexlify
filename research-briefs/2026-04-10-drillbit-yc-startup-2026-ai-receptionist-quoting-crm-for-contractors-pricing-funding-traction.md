# Drillbit (YC S24) — Research Brief
### AI Back-Office Automation for Residential Contractors

---

## Summary

Drillbit is a YC S24–backed startup (founded 2023, Austin TX) building an AI-powered back office for residential home-service contractors. Drillbit uses AI receptionists and an LLM-based quoting tool on top of its own CRM to entirely manage jobs from lead, to quote, to scheduling, to staffing, and finally payment. The company has raised at least $4.4 million, claims $95 M+ in GMV generated for clients, and reports 42% month-over-month growth. Pricing starts at $500/month/location with usage-based billing above quota. It is still early-stage (~14 employees) but is showing rapid traction in a $675 B+ U.S. home-maintenance market where incumbents like ServiceTitan and Jobber have not yet shipped comparable AI-native automation.

## Key Findings

### Product & Features
- **Core stack:** AI receptionists that can coordinate with each of the parties at every stage in the process, working with AI estimators to provide quotes, and together using Drillbit's CRM.
- **"Mason" AI employee:** Customers have been very happy with Mason. He is booking jobs at a rate 3x greater than the best human CSRs and driving enormous amounts of new revenue for clients.
- **Quoting engine:** Using a Large Language Model (LLM), the engine interprets job descriptions, breaks them into line items, and fills out pricing and descriptions automatically, significantly reducing the time and effort required to generate quotes.
- **Payments & collections:** Once a job is complete, Drillbit automatically requests payment from clients and can send follow-up reminders. Digital payment processing is built in, with low fees and no daily limits.
- **Integration approach:** Drillbit fits into your existing systems — no changes required. Think of us as another employee, rather than a new software system.
- **Security:** SOC2 compliant with verified data privacy and retention policies.

### Founders & Team
- Alex graduated from Stanford with a degree in CS & AI. After school, he worked as an engineer at SpaceX, Apple, Rivian, and Built Robotics. Alex is passionate about affordable housing, and after years of doing construction on the side, he merged his passions and started Drillbit.

### Pricing
- Drillbit starts at $500/month/location for a full-time AI employee that is more productive than entire departments of human CSRs. Usage-based billing kicks in after your employee has met their quota. Mason is guaranteed to produce at least $100 for every $1 you pay him.

### Traction & Performance Claims
- Mason, the AI employee, has generated over $95 M in GMV for customers across over 90 k opportunities. In the past 12 months, Drillbit has sustained 42% month-over-month growth, building a $98 M ARR sales pipeline.
- The homepage reports a **148% booking-rate increase**, **2.89× profit-margin increase**, and **$150 M+ overall client lift**.
- In just 90 days: ResiBrands saved $840 K annually, boosted profits by 2.56×, and added $5 M in new revenue — while simplifying operations across their network.
- We've seen an enormous increase in average profit margins from below 20% to over 60%.

### Market & Competitive Context
- Across the U.S., we spend about $675 B/year on simple home maintenance. A significant portion of that cost is attributed to the office work involved with finding, coordinating, and managing those small jobs.
- While platforms like Jobber and ServiceTitan have moved businesses from paper to digital, they still require significant manual input. Drillbit differentiates itself by leveraging AI to automate these processes completely.

## Open Questions

1. **Revenue vs. pipeline:** Job listings cite a "$98 M ARR sales pipeline," but actual recognized ARR/MRR is not disclosed. One third-party estimate (Extruct AI) shows ~$187 K in revenue — a very large gap that needs clarification.
2. **Valuation jump:** The valuation appears to have moved from $22 M to $50 M between two job-listing snapshots without a publicly announced round. Whether this reflects a priced round, new SAFE, or updated cap is unclear.
3. **GMV ≠ revenue:** $95 M+ GMV is value created *for customers*, not Drillbit's own take. The company's actual take rate / margin is not public.
4. **Competitive moat:** ServiceTitan (public, multi-billion-dollar valuation) and Jobber are shipping their own AI features. How defensible is Drillbit once incumbents catch up on AI receptionist and quoting capabilities?
5. **Customer concentration:** The ResiBrands case study is heavily featured; it is unclear how diversified the customer base is beyond a small number of large franchise networks.
6. **Homepage vs. job-listing metric divergence:** The homepage shows $150 M+ client lift / 110 k+ jobs; job listings cite $95 M GMV / 90 k opportunities. Timing or methodology differences are unexplained.
