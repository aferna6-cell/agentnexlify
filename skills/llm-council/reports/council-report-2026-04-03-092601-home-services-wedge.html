AgentNexLiFy Should Narrow to One Home-Services Wedge

The council was asked what AgentNexLiFy should do next as a business over the next 60-90 days, given a now-stable but very broad product surface and strong competitive pressure from better-capitalized horizontal players.

Question

Should AgentNexLiFy keep acting like a broad SMB automation suite, or should it narrow to a single vertical AI receptionist / lead-capture wedge with white-glove onboarding? If it narrows, what wedge and GTM motion should come first?

The Recommendation

Choose one sub-vertical in home services and sell AgentNexLiFy as an AI receptionist that answers, captures, qualifies, and books jobs 24/7. Do not keep leading with the broad SMB suite story.

The strongest starting lane is HVAC in one metro. The product already matches the pain, the ROI is obvious, and the buying motion can be founder-led and white-glove.

The One Thing to Do First

Write the paid pilot offer in one sentence:

We install an AI receptionist for HVAC companies that answers missed calls and web leads, qualifies them, and books more jobs within 30 days.

Then build a list of 10 HVAC owners in one metro and start outreach from that sentence.

Where the Council Agrees

  • Broad SMB suite positioning is a trap.
  • The next 60-90 days should be about one wedge, not more surface area.
  • The wedge should be phone-heavy home services.
  • The offer should focus on missed calls, booked jobs, and measurable ROI.
  • The GTM should be founder-led and white-glove, not self-serve.

Where the Council Clashes

Expansion Path

Keep a platform story in the background?

The Expansionist wants the wedge framed as an entry point into a bigger revenue layer. The harsher voices think that language is dangerous because it gives permission to drift back into feature sprawl too early.

GTM Texture

How heavy should the pilot motion be?

The council agrees on white-glove installs, but not on details: 10 vs 20 targets, setup-plus-monthly vs pilot-first pricing, and how fast to rewrite the entire message stack.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • Home services is still too broad. Pick one sub-vertical in one metro.
  • The offer boundary must be explicit. Lead with one promise, not the platform menu.
  • A hard validation rule is missing. Define paying-pilot count, time-to-live, and success thresholds before starting.
  • Founder capacity matters. White-glove onboarding is both the product and the bottleneck.
  • Distribution matters. The first wedge should be the one the founder can reach fastest, not just the one with abstract market logic.
