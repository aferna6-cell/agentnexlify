# Council Transcript - Home Services Wedge
**Date:** 2026-04-03
**Question:** What should AgentNexLiFy do next as a business over the next 60-90 days now that the codebase is more stable?

## Framed Question
AgentNexLiFy is an AI-powered SMB automation platform with a broad codebase: embeddable chat widget, lead capture, appointments, email/SMS sequences, CRM, invoices, reviews, webhooks, client portal, social media, SEO, and calls. The repo messaging is split: `README.md` still frames it around real estate, while `CLAUDE.md` frames it as a broader small-business automation platform. Pricing in repo docs spans free plus $249/$299/$499/$899 plans. Competitive context in the repo includes GoHighLevel, Drillbit, Phonely, Toma, Birdeye/Podium, and Oscar Chat.

Engineering context: the backend suite is green after bug/risk fixes, so the immediate question is no longer "can it run?" but "what business move should happen next?"

Recent council context from 2026-04-02 already converged on "stop building, start selling" and founder-led pilots. The unresolved decision is strategic focus:

Should AgentNexLiFy spend the next 60-90 days as:
- `A` one-vertical AI receptionist / lead-capture wedge with white-glove onboarding
- `B` a broad SMB automation suite

If `A`, what wedge and GTM motion should come first?

What's at stake: choosing wrong means burning more engineering time without revenue, staying overbuilt and under-positioned, and getting crushed by better-capitalized horizontal competitors.

## Advisor Responses

### Contrarian
A broad SMB suite is the trap. It gives you endless reasons to build and zero reasons for a buyer to care. Horizontal suites die because they're forgettable, expensive to explain, and impossible to outspend. You do not have the distribution to be the next GoHighLevel. Stop pretending you do.

Go hard on a single wedge: phone-first lead capture for one high-intent local vertical, starting with HVAC/plumbing/roofing. Not all contractors, not all SMB. One buyer, one promise: never miss a call, turn missed calls into booked jobs, and prove ROI in dollars within 7 days.

The GTM motion should be founder-led white-glove installs, not self-serve. Pick 10 target businesses in one metro, call them directly, and offer a done-for-you setup that answers calls, captures leads, and books appointments. You install manually, watch every call, fix every failure, and charge setup plus monthly. If they won't let you see the phone flow, they're not a customer.

Do not build more surface area. No social media, no SEO suite, no client portal polish, no invoices unless a pilot demands it. Those are feature graveyards. The business needs a sharp spear, not a box of tools.

### First Principles Thinker
Go with `A`, and cut harder than feels comfortable. The problem is not "build more software." The problem is that you need a category where the buyer instantly understands the pain, the ROI is obvious, and one conversation can close. A broad SMB suite fails first principles because it asks a small business to rewire too many habits at once. Nobody buys automation. They buy fewer missed calls, more booked jobs, and less front-desk chaos.

The first wedge should be home services, specifically HVAC/plumbing/roofing operators with inbound phone pressure. That wedge has the cleanest economics: every missed call is lost revenue, after-hours leads matter, and owners already feel the pain. Your current stack already matches it: widget, calls, SMS, appointments, reviews, webhooks. Don't market the platform. Market one outcome: AI receptionist that answers, qualifies, and books jobs 24/7.

GTM should be founder-led and brutally concrete:
1. Pick 20 local operators in one metro.
2. Offer white-glove setup in 48 hours on their real website and phone flow.
3. Sell a pilot, not a SaaS subscription first.
4. Measure missed calls recovered, booked appointments, and response time.
5. Turn the first win into a case study, then clone that exact motion.

If you keep the broad suite story, you will stay invisible. If you narrow to one pain, one buyer, and one promise, you can actually sell.

### Expansionist
Pursue `A`, but make the wedge bigger than a single feature. Don't sell chatbot software. Sell an AI revenue layer for high-intent local businesses that lose money every time a call is missed.

The best first wedge is home services, starting with one narrow lane like HVAC or plumbing. Why that lane: urgent calls, clear ROI, after-hours demand, easy appointment/booked-lead attribution, and enough operational pain that a founder will pay for white-glove setup without a long committee cycle. That lane also lets AgentNexLiFy expand naturally into calls, text-back, booking, quote capture, invoices, reviews, and follow-up sequences. It is not a tiny product. It is a platform entry point.

GTM should be founder-led and concierge-heavy:
1. Pick 10 target businesses in one metro.
2. Offer done-for-you setup in 48 hours.
3. Promise one metric: more booked jobs from missed calls and web leads.
4. Install the widget, call flow, and SMS follow-up as a single package.
5. Charge for implementation plus monthly subscription, then expand the account after the first win.

`B` is a trap. Broad SMB suite is where horizontal competitors live. A focused wedge creates proof, case studies, pricing power, and distribution. Once you win one lane, the broad suite becomes the expansion path, not the starting point.

### Outsider
`A`. Hard. The broad SMB suite is a trap. It sounds ambitious, but it is actually a refusal to choose a customer. Nobody wakes up wanting invoices, social media, SEO, CRM, and webhooks. They want fewer missed calls and more booked appointments.

If I were forcing one wedge from what's written here, I would pick AI receptionist plus lead capture for home services first, specifically plumbers/HVAC/roofing. That wedge is easy to understand, has immediate ROI, and matches the strongest parts of the product: widget, calls, appointments, SMS/email follow-up, and webhooks. It is also a clean contrast to GoHighLevel: not everything for everyone, but we answer, qualify, and book your leads.

The GTM motion should be founder-led and manual, not self-serve. Sell one offer: we install this on your site and phone so you stop losing leads. Use local outbound, warm intros, and short demos. Charge setup plus monthly. White-glove onboarding is not a service burden here. It is the product.

The mistake would be to keep adding horizontal features and hoping one day the market will assemble your positioning for you. It won't. Narrow the story, narrow the buyer, narrow the offer, then go get three real customers who feel the pain this week.

### Executor
Pursue `A`. Do not keep selling a broad SMB suite. That path is where you get ignored, compared to GoHighLevel, and forced into feature parity you cannot win. The business needs one sharp promise, one buyer, one demo, one outcome.

The best first wedge is home services, starting with HVAC/plumbing/roofing. They get high-intent calls and form fills, lose money on missed leads, and can understand ROI in one conversation. Your current stack already fits them: widget, calls, appointment booking, follow-up sequences, invoices, reviews, and lead capture. Don't invent a new category when the product already maps to one.

Monday morning motion:
1. Rewrite the homepage and deck to say one thing: AI receptionist that answers, captures, and books home-service leads 24/7.
2. Stop pitching features. Pitch missed calls, missed leads, booked jobs.
3. Pick 10 local operators and 10 more from one directory. Offer white-glove setup, not self-serve.
4. Close 3 pilots manually. Charge something even if it's small. Free pilots teach you nothing.
5. Record every live conversation, objection, and miss. Those become the product spec.

The only goal for the next 60-90 days is to prove: if we drop this on a real HVAC/plumbing shop, does it book more jobs? If yes, expand to adjacent home services. If no, change the wedge.

## Chairman's Synthesis

## Where the Council Agrees
The council is unusually aligned. AgentNexLiFy should not keep presenting as a broad SMB automation suite. The correct next move is `A`: a single painful wedge with founder-led, white-glove deployment. The wedge should be home services, and the message should center on one outcome: fewer missed calls, more booked jobs, faster response, measurable ROI.

Multiple advisors independently landed on the same buyer pattern: high-intent, phone-heavy local operators such as HVAC, plumbing, and roofing. The common reasoning is strong: missed calls are real lost revenue, urgency is obvious, ROI is easy to explain, and the current product already maps well to the job to be done.

## Where the Council Clashes
The biggest disagreement is not wedge choice. It is how much future-platform thinking is allowed right now.

The Expansionist wants the wedge framed as an "AI revenue layer" that can grow into a wider platform. The Contrarian and several reviewers think that language is dangerous because it gives permission to drift back into feature sprawl before the wedge has earned the right to expand.

There is also a softer disagreement on GTM texture: some push for 10 targets, some for 20; some prefer setup plus monthly immediately, others prefer a paid pilot first. Those differences matter operationally, but they are implementation details under a larger consensus: narrow the offer, charge early, and learn from live installs.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught
The advisors all converged on the wedge, but peer review exposed the missing business mechanics:

1. `Home services` is still too broad without a real first ICP. "HVAC/plumbing/roofing" is directionally right, but one sub-vertical in one metro is better than three.
2. The exact offer still needs a boundary. Is the first offer "AI receptionist," "missed-call recovery," or "lead capture + booking"? That needs to be stated in one sentence.
3. The council kept saying "pilots" without defining success and failure. A wedge without kill criteria is just a renamed drift.
4. Founder capacity matters. White-glove onboarding is the product for now, but it is also the bottleneck, so install volume must stay low and intentional.
5. Distribution advantage was under-specified. The best wedge is not just the one with the best economics; it is the one the founder can actually reach fastest.

## The Recommendation
Choose one sub-vertical in home services and make AgentNexLiFy an `AI receptionist that answers, captures, qualifies, and books jobs 24/7` for that buyer. Do not keep selling a broad SMB suite. Do not lead with CRM, SEO, invoices, social media, or "automation platform" language.

If forced to choose one starting lane, pick `HVAC` in one metro area. It has urgent inbound demand, clear booked-job economics, and a natural fit with the existing product. Plumbing is a close second, but the real discipline is not HVAC vs. plumbing. It is choosing exactly one first.

GTM for the next 60-90 days:
- rewrite the homepage, deck, and demo around that one promise
- offer a paid white-glove pilot, not free self-serve
- target 10-20 owners in one geography
- charge a setup fee plus monthly recurring fee, even if discounted
- define a pass/fail rule before starting: three paying pilots, live within 30 days, each producing measurable booked jobs or recovered leads inside the pilot window

No new surface area should be built unless a live pilot demands it. Expansion into the rest of the platform can happen later, but only after the wedge converts repeatedly.

## The One Thing to Do First
Write the exact paid pilot offer in one sentence for one buyer:

`We install an AI receptionist for HVAC companies that answers missed calls and web leads, qualifies them, and books more jobs within 30 days.`

Then build a list of 10 HVAC owners in one metro and start outreach from that sentence, not from the current platform story.
