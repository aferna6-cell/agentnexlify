# Cold-Outreach Templates + Partner Assignment

Closes launch-readiness rubric **9.5** (cold-outreach templates + partner
assignment). Voice follows `.claude/rules/personality.md` (direct, evidence-first,
no hype — no "unlock/leverage/seamless"). ICP: owner-operated local service
businesses (salons, plumbers, dental, contractors, med-spas) with a website but
no after-hours coverage.

Plans to reference: **chatbot** ($19.99/mo — AI Front Desk, widget/chat) ·
**agent_os** ($99.99/mo — AI Workforce, full platform).

---

## Partner assignment rules

| Lead source / type | Owner | SLA |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound `/contact` form + demo-widget chats | Partner A (sales) | first reply < 4 business h |
| Referral (`?ref=` signups) + existing-tenant expansion | Partner A | < 1 business day |
| Cold outbound (lists below) | Partner B (BD) | sequence cadence below |
| Vertical pilots (named design partners, e.g. MTOptions) | Founder | weekly check-in |
| Press / partnership inbound | Founder | < 1 business day |

- One owner per lead — set on capture; never two people emailing the same lead.
- Log every touch in the CRM with `next_step` + `due_date`. No silent drops.
- Move cold → warm on any reply; reassign to Partner A for close.

---

## Sequence 1 — Cold email (4 touches, 9 business days)

**Targeting:** business has a website + a contact form or booking page, no visible
live chat, listed hours that close before 6pm.

### Email 1 — Day 0 (the specific observation)
> **Subject:** after-hours messages on {{business_name}}'s site
>
> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> I looked at {{business_name}}'s site — when someone visits after {{close_time}}
> the only option is the contact form, and those usually get answered the next
> day. A chunk of those people book with whoever replies first.
>
> We put an AI front desk on the site that answers questions, captures the lead,
> and books the appointment 24/7. Setup is one script tag. $19.99/mo.
>
> Worth a 10-minute look? I can show it answering a real question about your
> business.
>
> {{sender_name}}

### Email 2 — Day 3 (proof, not a nudge)
> **Subject:** re: after-hours messages on {{business_name}}'s site
>
> Quick follow-up — here's the widget answering a {{vertical}} question live:
> {{demo_link}}. It pulls from your services and hours, not a generic script.
>
> If the front desk earns its keep, the $99.99 plan adds the rest of the staff
> (follow-ups, quotes, review requests). Start at $19.99 and move up only if it
> works. 10 minutes this week?

### Email 3 — Day 6 (one number)
> **Subject:** {{business_name}} — one number
>
> Most owners we talk to lose 3–5 after-hours leads a week to slow replies. At
> your average ticket that's real money walking. The widget closes that gap for
> less than one missed job a month. Want me to set up a free test on a staging
> copy of your page?

### Email 4 — Day 9 (break-up, leave the door open)
> **Subject:** closing the loop
>
> I'll stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If after-hours coverage ever
> moves up your list, reply and I'll spin up a test the same day. Demo stays here
> if useful: {{demo_link}}.

**Rules:** stop the sequence on any reply (hand to Partner A). Never send touch 2+
if touch 1 bounced. Personalize `{{close_time}}` / `{{vertical}}` per lead — no
mail-merge that reads like mail-merge.

## Sequence 2 — LinkedIn / DM (3 touches)

   $19.99/mo. Happy to show it on your actual site, no pitch call needed."
3. **Day 6:** share `{{demo_link}}` with one line tying it to their vertical.

## Objection one-liners (keep handy, stay honest)

- **"We already have a chatbot."** Good — does it book appointments and hand you
  the lead, or just answer FAQs? Ours does the booking. Test it next to yours.
- **"Too expensive."** It's $19.99 to start — less than one missed job. Move up
  only if it pays for itself.
- **"We don't get much web traffic."** Then the few visitors you get matter more,
  not less — and you'll see exactly how many it captures.
- **"Is it just GoHighLevel?"** No — widget-first, per-business knowledge, lower
  friction. We're not selling you an agency platform to learn.

## Do-not list
- No fake scarcity, no "quick question" subject lines, no "circling back."
- Never claim a customer/result we don't have (see rubric 8.5 — no case study yet).
- Don't disparage competitors by name beyond the factual `/help` comparison.
