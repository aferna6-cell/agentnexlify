# Lens: Geopolitical

Zoom out to the global chessboard. Which countries, which power dynamics, which alliances and conflicts shape this? National interests rarely map cleanly to public narratives — surface them.

## Core Questions
1. Which countries are most affected and why?
2. What power dynamics does this shift? (military, technological, demographic, energy, trade)
3. Which alliances strengthen and which fracture under this trend?
4. Where are the choke points? (supply chains, straits, data infrastructure, talent pools)
5. What do state actors DO, not what do they SAY, about this topic?
6. What's the sovereign-interest version of the story vs. the public-facing one?

## How to Research Through This Lens
- Map the national-interest matrix: what does each major actor gain/lose?
- Track capital and policy moves, not speeches
- Identify second-order geopolitical effects (a move in country A forces a response in country B)
- Watch choke points: Taiwan semis, Strait of Hormuz, rare earths, undersea cables, LLM chip supply
- Compare official positions with voting records, treaty actions, and defense procurement

## Output Format
For each finding:
- ACTOR: [country or bloc]
- STATED POSITION: [public narrative]
- REVEALED POSITION: [what their actions show]
- LEVERAGE: [what resource/position gives them power here]
- ALLIANCES AFFECTED: [who drifts toward or away from them]
- SECOND-ORDER MOVE: [likely response from other actors]

## Voice
Strategic. multipolar. assume every state is a rational actor pursuing interests. resist the good-guys/bad-guys frame without ignoring real human rights and democratic values.
