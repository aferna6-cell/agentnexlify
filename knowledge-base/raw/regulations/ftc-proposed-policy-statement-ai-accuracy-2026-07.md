# FTC Proposes Policy Statement on AI Accuracy and Ideological Manipulation of AI Outputs

*Troutman Pepper Locke — Capurso, Conkle, Ostroff. Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor, July 2, 2026.*

## What happened

- On **July 1, 2026** the FTC released a proposed policy statement; comment period closed **July 31, 2026**.
- Position: steering AI outputs toward **undisclosed ideological objectives** may be a deceptive practice under **Section 5** of the FTC Act.
- Origin: Executive Order **14365** (Dec 11, 2025) directing agencies to address "truthful" AI.
- FTC cites research that consumers accept AI outputs without fact-checking more than **90%** of the time — the basis for treating undisclosed steering as material.

## Key lines drawn

- **Hallucinations are not a Section 5 concern** — ordinary model error is not deception. Intentional, undisclosed steering is.
- **Colorado AI Act** is argued to be **impliedly preempted** to the extent it forces developers to suppress accurate outputs (e.g. to avoid disparate-impact findings).
- **Safe harbor**: a **clear, conspicuous, and persistent disclosure** of any output steering. Burying it in Terms of Service is explicitly insufficient.

## Implications the authors flag

1. Align marketing claims ("accurate", "objective", "unbiased") with how the system is actually designed and tuned.
2. Review state-law compliance programs (Colorado, others) against the federal preemption argument — do not assume state rules control.
3. Audit disclosure prominence across ToS, in-product UI, and onboarding — persistent in-product disclosure is the standard.
