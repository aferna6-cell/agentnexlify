# California Companion Chatbot Law Now in Effect

**Date:** January 12, 2026
**Authors:** Kirk A. Soderquist, Divya Taneja, D. Sean West
**Firm:** Perkins Coie LLP

## Overview
California Senate Bill 243 became effective January 1, 2026 — the first comprehensive regulatory framework for "companion chatbots." Moves beyond disclosure requirements to operational safety protocols and reporting obligations.

## Scope and Exclusions
Targets chatbots designed to "engage users in ways that can meet social or emotional needs" through adaptive, human-like responses sustaining relationships across multiple interactions.

**Excluded:**
- Customer service and business operations chatbots
- Video game chatbots limited to game-related discussions
- Voice-activated assistants without sustained multi-interaction relationships

## Core Requirements

### Disclosure
Operators must clearly notify users they're interacting with AI if a reasonable person might believe otherwise.

### Safety Protocols
Operators must "implement a protocol designed to prevent the chatbot from producing content related to suicidal ideation, suicide, or self-harm." Minimums:
- Referrals to crisis services
- Published safety details on company websites

### Minor Protections
When operators know users are minors:
- Disclose AI interaction status
- Provide break reminders every 3 hours by default
- Prevent sexually explicit content or encouragement

## Reporting
From July 1, 2027, operators submit annual reports to California's Office of Suicide Prevention documenting crisis referrals and safety protocols implemented.

## Enforcement
Private right of action — users can sue for violations seeking injunctive relief, damages, attorney fees.

## Relevance for AgentNexLiFy
- AgentNexLiFy widget chatbot is "customer service / business operations" → likely **excluded** from SB 243 scope
- Confirm with counsel before any feature that could read as "companion" (long-context relationship building, emotional support framing)
- AI-disclosure best practice still applies (TCPA-adjacent + FTC guidance), even if SB 243 doesn't bind us
- Crisis-content protocol is relevant if widget ever interacts with end-customers in healthcare/dental/medical office vertical
