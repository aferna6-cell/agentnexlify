# 2026 State Chatbot Laws: Key Provisions and Regulatory Trends

**Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe · April 29, 2026**

## Overview

With comprehensive federal AI legislation stalled, states have moved into the gap. The 2026 legislative sessions produced a wave of chatbot-specific statutes. For any business deploying a customer-facing conversational AI, the compliance surface is now a state-by-state patchwork rather than a single standard.

The laws cluster into three categories: **disclosure requirements**, **companion-AI safeguards**, and **sector-specific restrictions** (health, mental health, insurance, employment).

## 1. Disclosure Requirements — The Broadest Category

The common core across states: a consumer interacting with an AI system must be told they are not talking to a human.

Recurring provisions:

- **Clear and conspicuous disclosure** at the outset of the interaction
- **Disclosure on request** — if a user asks whether they are talking to a bot, the system must say yes
- **No deceptive human impersonation** — prohibiting AI presenting itself as a person, including through names, avatars, or claims
- **Human escalation path** in several statutes — a mechanism to reach a person

**California** established the earliest template (the "Bolstering Online Transparency" framework) making it unlawful to use a bot to communicate with a person in California to incentivize a sale or influence a vote without disclosing it is a bot. The 2026 wave broadens this from commercial and electoral contexts to general consumer interaction.

**Utah's AI Policy Act** requires disclosure when asked in general consumer interactions, and **proactive** disclosure for regulated occupations. Notably, Utah closed the "the AI did it" defense — a business cannot escape existing consumer protection liability by attributing the conduct to generative AI.

**Maine** enacted a chatbot disclosure statute treating undisclosed AI interaction as a deceptive trade practice.

Timing matters: most disclosure statutes require the notice **before or at the start** of the interaction, not buried in a terms-of-service link.

## 2. Companion AI and Minor Safeguards

The fastest-moving category, driven by high-profile harm incidents involving minors and companion chatbots.

Typical requirements:

- **Crisis protocols** — detection of suicidal ideation or self-harm content with referral to crisis resources
- **Periodic AI reminders** — recurring notices during extended sessions (commonly every few hours) that the user is talking to a machine
- **Restrictions on sexual content** with known minors
- **Reporting obligations** — some statutes require operators to report crisis referral statistics to a state office
- **Age-appropriate design duties** where minors are reasonably likely to be users

The definitional question that drives applicability: what counts as a "companion" chatbot. Most statutes define it around sustained, relationship-like, human-mimicking interaction. **Customer service and transactional bots are generally carved out** — but the carve-out language varies, and a bot with a persona and open-ended conversation is closer to the line than a booking assistant.

## 3. Sector-Specific Restrictions

- **Mental health** — several states restrict AI from providing therapy or mental-health treatment without licensed human oversight; **Illinois** enacted among the most restrictive provisions
- **Health care** — disclosure requirements for AI-generated clinical communications; some states require a licensed provider to review AI-generated clinical content
- **Insurance** — restrictions on AI-driven claims and coverage decisions without human review
- **Employment** — notice and, in some jurisdictions, bias-audit requirements for AI used in hiring

## Enforcement and Penalties

- Primary enforcement is by **state attorneys general**, typically under existing unfair-and-deceptive-practices authority
- Penalties are commonly assessed **per violation**, and a "violation" can mean per consumer interaction — creating meaningful aggregate exposure at scale
- Some statutes include **private rights of action**, particularly in the minor-protection category
- Cure periods exist in some states but are not universal

## Practical Compliance Steps

1. **Inventory every consumer-facing conversational AI**, including embedded widgets, SMS bots, and voice agents
2. **Implement clear disclosure at the start of every interaction**, in the interface itself
3. **Ensure the bot answers truthfully when asked if it is AI** — this is a distinct requirement from opening disclosure and is frequently missed
4. **Build a human escalation path** and make it discoverable
5. **Determine whether any deployment meets a "companion AI" definition** in states where you have users; if so, add crisis protocols and periodic reminders
6. **Map your user geography** — obligations attach to the consumer's state, not the business's
7. **Log disclosures** so compliance is provable after the fact
8. **Review sector rules** if operating in health, mental health, insurance, or employment
9. **Update vendor contracts** to allocate responsibility for disclosure compliance
10. **Monitor continuously** — the patchwork is expanding each session

## Trends to Watch

- **Convergence on disclosure** as the baseline requirement across nearly every state that has acted
- **Divergence on companion AI** — definitions and safeguard requirements vary substantially
- **Expansion beyond text** to voice agents, which many early statutes did not clearly cover
- **Federal preemption pressure** — proposals to preempt state AI law have been introduced but not enacted; absent preemption, the patchwork deepens
- **Shift from disclosure to design duties** — later-session bills increasingly impose affirmative safety-design obligations rather than only notice

## Bottom Line

Disclosure is now table stakes and cheap to implement. The higher-risk exposure is in companion-AI classification and sector-specific rules. Businesses deploying customer-service or booking chatbots should implement disclosure and escalation immediately, then assess whether any product surface drifts toward a companion-AI definition in the states where their users live.
