# TCPA Text Messages: Rules and Regulations Guide for 2026

**Published:** June 12, 2026
**Author:** Andrew Bailey

## Core TCPA Requirements for Text Messages

### 1. Clear Disclosures
Before obtaining consent, businesses must provide transparent disclosures stating:
- "Explicitly stating that the recipient agrees to receive automated marketing messages" from a named company
- The message categories consumers will receive
- Potential charges (message and data rates)
- An option to revoke consent anytime

### 2. Prior Express Written Consent (PEWC)
Marketing text messages require **prior express written consent**. Simple call-to-action responses (e.g., "Text SAVE to 54321") have faced litigation challenges and may not constitute valid consent. Compliant consent must:
- Be presented clearly and unambiguously through web forms or robust text-to-join programs
- State consent is not a purchase condition
- Specify the phone number and message types

### 3. Clear Opt-Out Mechanisms
- "Clear instructions on how to stop receiving messages (e.g., replying 'STOP')"
- Processing opt-outs within **10 business days**
- No promotional messages after opt-out requests
- Only final confirmation messages allowed (without promotional content)

### 4. Record-Keeping Requirements
Maintain auditable documentation: date, time, and collection method of consent; exact consent language presented; recipient contact information. "Tools like TrustedForm can automate this process by securely documenting and storing proof of consent."

### 5. Additional Requirements

**Time-of-Day Restrictions:** Messages prohibited **before 8 am and after 9 pm** in recipient's time zone; many states enforce stricter limits; nationwide campaigns must adjust per time zone.

**Do-Not-Call Compliance:** Scrub against Federal DNC Registry regularly; check state DNC registries; verify numbers against Reassigned Number Database (RND) before sending.

**Penalties:** **$500–$1,500 per violation** statutory damages; class action risk; FCC enforcement exposure.

## TCPA Text Message Exemptions

| Type | Examples | Consent Required |
|----------|------------|----------------------|
| Informational (non-marketing) | Appointment reminders, delivery notifications, account updates, password resets | Prior Express Consent (PEC) |
| Emergency messages | Severe weather alerts, public safety warnings, lockdown notifications | None |
| Healthcare (HIPAA-regulated) | Appointment confirmations, prescription notifications, pre-op instructions | Prior Express Consent (PEC) |
| Purely non-commercial | Political messages, nonprofit outreach, surveys, advocacy | Prior Express Consent (typically) |

"Seek legal counsel or a TCPA compliance expert" before claiming exemptions.

## Informational vs. Marketing Messages

- **Informational:** appointment reminders, shipping updates, account notifications → Prior Express Consent (lower standard); may be collected orally or via business card sharing.
- **Marketing:** discount offers, product announcements → Prior Express **Written** Consent (higher standard); must explicitly state agreement to receive marketing messages.

## Consent Language Examples

"Use of this example language by itself in a campaign is not enough to present TCPA compliant consent. Seek review by your legal and compliance functions."

**Standard Marketing Example:**
"By checking this box, you agree to receive recurring automated promotional and personalized marketing text messages (e.g., cart reminders) from [Your Company] at the mobile number provided. Consent is not a condition of purchase. Msg & data rates may apply. Msg frequency varies. Reply HELP for help, STOP to cancel."

**Informational Example:**
"By providing your number, you consent to receive automated service-related messages (e.g., appointment reminders) from [Your Company]."

## Related search context (2026-07-23)

- On January 24, 2025, the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent rule in Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC, finding the FCC exceeded its statutory authority. As of 2026, one-to-one consent is no longer a requirement.
- Two systems govern texts: TCPA (federal law, FCC-enforced) and CTIA guidelines (carrier rules enforced by major carriers).
