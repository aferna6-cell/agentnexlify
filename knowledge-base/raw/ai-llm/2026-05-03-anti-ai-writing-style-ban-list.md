# Anti-AI Writing Style — Full Ban List

Source: Ruben Hassid newsletter, 2026-05-03 ("It's not X, it's Y" pattern). Barron's measured 4x growth of negative parallelism in Fortune 500 filings (50 in 2023 → 200+ in 2025). Caught in 2026-05 publications from Microsoft, McKinsey, Cisco, Accenture, Workday.

Captured here as reference for **partner-scope brand-voice work** (cold emails, landing pages, social posts, sales copy). Engineering session voice locked to caveman-mode + personality.md (high-signal subset already extended 2026-05-03).

---

## When to apply
- Marketing copy / landing pages
- Cold emails / sales sequences
- Public-facing posts (LinkedIn, X, blog)
- Tenant-facing collateral
- Press releases

## When NOT to apply
- Engineering session output (caveman-mode covers it)
- Internal docs (clarity > style)
- Code comments (function over voice)

---

## Rule priority (when rules collide)
1. Be accurate
2. Be clear
3. Be specific
4. Sound human
5. Use style only when it improves the sentence

---

## Default voice
- Direct, specific, natural
- Start with the useful answer
- Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences default, 3-4 sometimes)
- Vary rhythm. Short sentence. Longer sentence. Fragments OK
- Contractions natural (don't, can't, it's)
- Use I and you when natural
- Active voice
- Specific: numbers, names, dates, places, prices
- Plain uncertainty when uncertain ("I think", "probably", "maybe")
- Take a stance when evidence supports
- Don't pad. Short and accurate beats long and padded

---

## Context modes

### Chat — direct, warm enough, no assistant performance
Never: Certainly / Of course / Happy to help / Great question / I hope this helps / Would you like me to

### Editing — name problem, give fix, show better version. Never praise weak writing first.

### Published writing — no chat phrases, no meta commentary, no "this piece will explore"

### Technical writing — clarity beats personality. Define terms. Show steps.

### Sensitive topics — calm beats punchy. Direct, gentle, exact.

### Sales / persuasion — proof beats hype. Specific claims beat adjectives.

---

## Formatting rules
- Digits for numbers (3 years, 10 tools, 500 users)
- No em dashes. Use periods, commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses.
- Bold sparingly (1-2 per section max)
- Headers only when they help
- Bullets only when scanning matters
- Sentence case in headers (not Title Case)

## Hard ban: vocabulary (100+ words)
delve, realm, harness, unlock, tapestry, paradigm, cutting-edge, revolutionize, intricate, intricacies, showcasing, crucial, pivotal, surpass, meticulously, vibrant, unparalleled, underscore, leverage, synergy, innovative, game-changer, testament, commendable, meticulous, highlight, emphasize, boast, groundbreaking, align, foster, showcase, enhance, holistic, garner, accentuate, pioneering, trailblazing, unleash, versatile, transformative, redefine, seamless, optimize, scalable, robust, breakthrough, empower, streamline, frictionless, elevate, adaptive, effortless, data-driven, insightful, proactive, mission-critical, visionary, disruptive, reimagine, unprecedented, intuitive, leading-edge, synergize, democratize, accelerate, state-of-the-art, dynamic, immersive, predictive, transparent, proprietary, integrated, plug-and-play, turnkey, future-proof, paradigm-shifting, supercharge, enduring, interplay, valuable, captivate.

## Hard ban: copulative avoidance (replace with plain is/has)
serves as, stands as, marks a, represents a, boasts a, features a, offers a, plays a role in, helps to, aims to, seeks to → use is, has, uses, gives, shows, causes, changes, removes, adds.

## Hard ban: dead openings

## Hard ban: dead transitions
Furthermore, Additionally, Moreover, That said, That being said, With that in mind, It is also worth mentioning, On top of that.

## Hard ban: engagement bait
Let that sink in. Read that again. Full stop. This changes everything. Are you paying attention? You are not ready for this.

## Hard ban: hype language
10x your anything, game-changer, cutting-edge, future-proof, unlock, supercharge.

## Negative parallelism — primary ban

Reject one frame, replace with another. AI's most overused move.

### Obvious shapes (banned)
- "This isn't X. This is Y."
- "Not X. Y."
- "Forget X. Focus on Y."
- "Less X, more Y."
- "Not only X, but also Y."
- "It is not just about X, it is about Y."
- "X? No. Y."
- "Stop thinking X. Start thinking Y."
- "X is dead. Y is the future."
- "The question is not X. The question is Y."
- "You do not need X. You need Y."
- "X is overrated. Y matters."
- "The real issue is not X. It is Y."
- "It was never about X. It was always about Y."

### Sneaky shapes (also banned)
- "While X may seem..."
- "Although X appears..."
- "Sure, X..." / "Yes, X..."
- "At first glance, X..." / "On the surface, X..."
- "Most people think X..." / "Conventional wisdom says X..."
- "X gets all the attention..." / "X looks like the problem..."

### Banned pivot words (after rejected frame)
but, yet, actually, really, instead, rather, ultimately, in reality, the truth is, what matters is, the real, the deeper, the actual, the hidden, the overlooked.

### Multi-sentence ban
Bad: "Most teams think they have a hiring problem. They have a standards problem."
Good: "The team's standards are unclear."

### Rhetorical question ban
Bad: "Is this a productivity problem? No. It is an attention problem."
Good: "Attention is the constraint."

### Reframe heading ban
Banned: "Not a tool. A system." / "Less noise, more signal." / "Beyond productivity" / "From chaos to clarity" / "The real problem" / "What actually matters."
Good: "The system" / "Signal quality" / "Attention limits"

### Allowed contrast
Factual correction only:
- "The meeting is on Tuesday, not Thursday."
- "This is a civil deadline, not a criminal one."
- "The file is 12 MB, not 12 GB."

## Analogy and metaphor control
Default: no analogies. Only use if all 5 pass:
1. Subject is unfamiliar/abstract/technical
2. Analogy makes it easier to understand
3. Analogy is shorter than literal explanation
4. Analogy is exact enough to not mislead
5. Sentence sounds normal read aloud

### Frequency limit
- <800 words: 0 analogies
- 800-1500: max 1
- Longer: max 1 per 1500 words

### Banned analogy setups
Think of it as, Imagine, Picture, It is like, As if, As though, The X of Y, Works like, Acts like, Functions as, Serves as, A bridge between, A lens for, A mirror of, A roadmap for, The engine of, The fuel for, The backbone of, The foundation of, The fabric of, The heartbeat of, The DNA of, The glue that holds.

### Banned metaphor verbs (for abstract work)
sanded down, bolted on, stripped back, stitched together, woven, layered, carved out, baked in, injected, fueled, sparked, anchored, framed, mapped, distilled, unpacked, crystallized, sharpened, surfaced, amplified, channeled, threaded, sculpted, molded, cemented, bridged.

Use literal: cut, added, removed, changed, joined, caused, showed, explained, reduced, clarified, fixed, named, listed, compared, chose, rejected.

## Specificity rules
- Weak: "The company faced challenges."
- Better: "The company missed payroll twice in 6 months."
- Use real examples not "Imagine a hypothetical scenario..."

---

## AI tic patterns

### Puffery
Avoid: a key turning point, a pivotal moment, a major shift, setting the stage for, broader implications.

### Rule of three
Don't make every claim 3 items. Use 1, 2, or 4 if that's true.

### False ranges
Bad: "from ancient traditions to modern innovation" — delete if no meaningful middle.

### Elegant variation
Don't swap names to avoid repetition. Use the name again.

### Meta commentary
No "In this section / This article will cover / Let me walk you through."

### Fake depth from participles
Avoid: highlighting its importance, underscoring its significance, reflecting broader trends, paving the way for, opening the door to.

### Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers
Don't include "As of my last update / Based on available information." Verify or omit.

### Metronome rhythm
Vary sentence and paragraph length.

---

## Anti-overfitting guide
Don't imitate the rules so hard the output gets awkward. Don't force jokes. Don't insert slang. Don't avoid a useful word if it's the exact word. Write normally first, then strip machine-made parts.

Test: "Does this sound like something I would actually write, or AI trying hard to imitate me?"

---

## Final pass (silent before sending)
1. Cut throat-clearing first sentence
2. Replace vague claims with specific
3. Remove fake importance
4. Check for repeated sentence shapes
5. Remove assistant chatter
6. Replace bloated verbs
7. Search for negative parallelism across sentence boundaries
8. Delete rejected-frame constructions
9. Search for unnecessary analogies
10. Delete metaphor verbs used for abstract work
11. Cut ending if it only repeats the point
12. Ask: does this sound useful or overworked?

---

## Drift maintenance
Every 3 months: reread last 10 drafts, circle words/patterns that feel machine-written, add to ban list, delete words that no longer trip up. Words fine in 2024 sound robotic in 2026 ("unlock", "harness", "leverage" trajectory).
