 By Diego Valverde | Journalist & Industry Analyst - 
 
 Mon, 04/13/2026 - 12:10 
 

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

Henkel Mexico and Azurally highlight a shift from SEO to GEO, where brands optimize for AI-generated answers and citations. In Mexico, this can impact digital marketing, media, and e-commerce sectors, requiring structured, authoritative content to maintain visibility across AI platforms.

 

The world is witnessing a transition from search engine optimization (SEO) to generative engine optimization (GEO), as traditional search engines now prioritize synthesizing direct answers over displaying a list of website links. Through the Fester GEO case study, Henkel Mexico and Azurally show how brands are transitioning from traditional search models to an omni-search ecosystem to maintain and optimize their visibility in AI interfaces.

The case study argues that the winner is not the brand with the best position in a list, but the one that provides the best answer and is cited by AI as the definitive authority. This shift is forcing companies to move beyond simple keyword optimization. According to Azurally’s research, search habits have evolved into an omni-search model, where users interact with multiple platforms, including social media, marketplaces, and generative tools, to complete a single journey.

The 2026 Transition from SEO to GEO

The digital marketing landscape in 2026 faces a paradigm shift driven by the consolidation of GEO. In previous years, SEO focused primarily on Google and Bing. However, the customer journey is now diversified among various platforms such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Copilot. This new environment defines the omni-search, where users investigate, compare, and decide through different channels simultaneously.

A critical factor in this evolution is the rise of "Zero Click Results," which occurs when an AI engine provides a complete answer within the search interface, removing the need for the user to visit an external website.  

While this trend has led to a decrease in organic traffic for many technical websites during 2025, the Fester case study shows that high-quality content can still achieve double-digit growth in organic visibility. By focusing on becoming a cited source for Google AI Overviews, Fester doubled its volume of impressions.

The transition from SEO to GEO means that the brand digital footprint must be legible for machine learning models. Traditional SEO metrics, such as clicks and click-through rates, are no longer the primary indicators of success. Instead, brands must prioritize authority and relevance to ensure that generative engines use their data to fuel AI responses. The objective is to ensure that when a user asks a complex question, the AI provides a response based on the brand’s technical data and proprietary information. 

Fester relies on a technical methodology that addresses the requirements of modern large language models (LLM’s). The strategy focuses on three core pillars: content structure, authority building, and omni-search optimization (OSO).

Content Structuring for Machine Readability

To optimize for generative engines, the companies argue that content must be divided into clear, easy-to-extract fragments. This process, known as "content chunking," allows an AI to identify specific data points to construct an answer. Fester implemented the following technical adjustments:

	
- 
	

Use of hierarchical headings and structured lists to define information priority.
	
	
- 
	

Implementation of advanced schema markup to provide semantic context to search crawlers.
	
	
- 
	

Creation of data-heavy tables that generative models can easily process for comparisons.
	
	
- 
	

Writing in natural language to match the conversational tone of AI prompts.
	

These adjustments align with the March 2026 Core Update from Google, which reinforced a quality-first ranking model. This update penalizes generic or scaled AI content while rewarding demonstrable experience and proprietary data. Henkel notes that content must now work as both an organic result and an AI Overview source, as these functions can no longer be treated separately.

New Measurement Framework: GEO KPIs

The decline of traditional metrics necessitated the development of new key performance indicators (KPIs) to evaluate GEO strategies. Azurally utilized specific technology to monitor the positioning of Fester in generative environments using four primary metrics:

	
- 
	

Visibility Score: The percentage of brand visibility within the monitored generative searches.
	
	
- 
	

Mentions: The total number of brand detections within a specific period.
	
	
- 
	

Citations: The number of times the AI provides a reference or link to the brand as a source of truth.
	
	
- 
	

Sentiment: The average tone of the AI responses when referencing the brand.
	

By tracking these indicators, Fester achieved a status as a top GEO authority in Google AI Mode. The data indicates that applying GEO strategies can increase visibility in generative answers by about 40%. This visibility is crucial because AI models favor content that is cited by authoritative external sources rather than self-published promotional material.

The Evolution Toward OSO

The OSO approach optimizes the brand presence across all digital spaces where a search occurs. Because users do not search in a single location, Fester expanded its strategy to include social media, video platforms, and technical forums.

Eduardo López and Teresa Flores, Partners, Azurally, say that the goal is to optimize the entire digital ecosystem. This involves creating multi-format content that resolves specific problems for the user. Instead of focusing on high-volume keywords, the content focuses on "helpful content logic," ensuring that the information is useful for both the human user and the generative engine.

Public Relay notes that strategy for media teams now must focus on:

	
- 
	

Conducting specific visibility audits across different AI engines, such as Gemini and ChatGPT, as each engine interprets data differently.
	
	
- 
	

Building external authority through collaborations with reputable media to generate high-quality backlinks.
	
	
- 
	

Prioritizing "extractable" content that AI can easily select and reuse in synthesized answers.
