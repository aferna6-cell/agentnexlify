# GoHighLevel Pricing Teardown 2026 — Base Plans, Usage Fees, and Real Agency Math

GoHighLevel's public pricing page shows three plans — Starter $97, Unlimited $297, Agency Pro $497 — but these headline numbers materially understate the real monthly cost of running the platform. Every meaningful outbound action (SMS, voice call, email send, AI generation) is metered on top of the base plan, and the AI Employee bundle documented in [[ghl-ai-employee-platform-reselling]] is an additional $97/mo per sub-account. A single-operator Starter account lands around $107/mo in realistic use, a 10-client Unlimited agency around $412/mo, and a 10-client SaaS Mode Agency Pro running AI Employee Unlimited across every client lands at approximately $1,591/mo in platform cost before any revenue. This teardown documents what each tier actually covers and where the usage-fee stack lives — because the pricing comparison against AgentNexLiFy and other competitors only makes sense with all of it visible.

The three plans differ on sub-account ceilings and SaaS Mode access, not on feature depth. Starter ($97/mo) includes the full platform — same CRM, funnels, automations, email marketing, booking calendar, reputation management, course/community hosting, and 24/7 support as every higher tier — capped at 3 sub-accounts. Unlimited ($297/mo) removes the sub-account cap, adds a branded desktop app and API access, and is the agency-standard tier where per-client platform cost drops to $14.85/mo at 20 clients. Agency Pro ($497/mo) adds SaaS Mode (automated client billing via Stripe), a white-label mobile app, a white-label Zapier equivalent, and snapshot-based instant client deployment. Annual billing drops effective monthly rates to approximately $81/$248/$414 — about two months free per year. The breakeven math for SaaS Mode is 2 clients at $297/mo; every additional client after that is near-pure recurring margin, which is why agencies aggressively upgrade once they land client number three.

The usage-fee stack is where GHL's published pricing and real cost diverge by 10-100%. LC Phone (LeadConnector Phone) charges approximately $1.15/mo per local number, $2.15/mo per toll-free number, $0.0079/segment for US SMS (both directions), $0.014/min outbound local calls, and $0.0085/min inbound calls — with carrier fee increases in effect from April 1 (AT&T) and May 1 (Verizon) 2026, documented separately in [[ghl-carrier-pricing-april-2026]]. A2P 10DLC registration is mandatory for US business SMS at $4-$19 per brand one-time; without registration, carrier filters block messages entirely. LC Email sits at $0.675 per 1,000 outbound sends and $2.50 per 1,000 email verifications. Workflow Pro tiers ($25/mo Starter, $99/mo Growth) gate premium triggers and actions like inbound webhooks, AI-branched steps, and complex conditional logic; simple linear automations stay free. WordPress hosting is $5/mo per site.

AI pricing deserves its own column. Pay-as-you-go rates are $0.02 per Conversation AI message, approximately $0.163/min for Voice AI (voice engine $0.06/min plus LLM token processing), $0.09 per 1,000 words for Content AI, $0.06 per AI-generated image, $0.08 per automated Reviews AI response, $0.99 per Funnel AI page build, and approximately $0.02 per Workflow AI optimization run. The alternative is AI Employee Unlimited at $97/mo per sub-account covering Conversation AI, Reviews AI, Content AI, and Funnel AI with no per-action caps — but Voice AI remains usage-based even on the unlimited plan. At scale the two pricing models converge: the source cites an agency running 10 clients at 200 calls/mo averaging 3 minutes, generating 6,000 voice minutes at $0.163/min = ~$978/mo, almost identical to the $970/mo for 10 subs on unlimited AI Employee. The real difference is predictability, not cost.

Three realistic monthly-cost scenarios published in the source illustrate the stack. Scenario 1 (solo Starter, 1 phone number, 500 SMS, 2,000 emails): $107/mo, modest markup on the $97 headline. Scenario 2 (Unlimited agency, 10 clients, 10 phone numbers, 5,000 SMS, 20,000 emails, moderate AI usage): $412/mo — 39% over the $297 headline. Scenario 3 (Agency Pro, 10 clients on AI Employee Unlimited, 10 phone numbers, 10,000 SMS, 50,000 emails): $1,591/mo, 3.2x the $497 headline. Scenario 3 grosses $3,970/mo at $397/client × 10 clients for a 60% net margin, which is the actual SaaS Mode economics agencies quote internally. The positioning that GHL replaces 6-10 tools at 30-60% lower total spend holds only when the buyer is comparing against HubSpot ($800+), ActiveCampaign + Calendly + Twilio ($180+), or Podium + CRM ($300-$450) — not against specialized single-purpose tools where a small-volume user might genuinely spend less on a narrower stack.

Competitive framing matters for AgentNexLiFy. AgentNexLiFy's `agent_os` tier ($99.99/mo; pricing updated 2026-06-15) sits well below GHL's Unlimited base plan, with the real comparison being $99.99 flat vs. $412/mo realistic GHL total (Scenario 2) — a ~75% price advantage before any feature comparison. The prior $249/$499 AgentNexLiFy tier references in this analysis predate the 2026-06-15 repricing. [[gohighlevel]] documents the plan tiers; [[ghl-ai-employee-platform-reselling]] explains why per-sub AI rebilling is a structural advantage that single-price AgentNexLiFy plans can't match without building reseller tooling. The April 2026 feature release in [[ghl-april-2026-product-updates]] adds Workflow AI Builder and Ask AI on top of this pricing stack, raising the bar on what $297/mo is expected to include.

## Key Concepts

- **LC Phone (LeadConnector Phone)** — GHL's Twilio-backed telecom layer charging per number, per SMS segment, and per call minute on top of the base plan. Rates rise April-May 2026 per carrier fee increases.
- **A2P 10DLC** — Application-to-Person 10-digit long code registration mandatory for US business SMS. $4-$19 one-time per brand; non-registered traffic is filtered by carriers.
- **SaaS Mode** — Agency Pro feature enabling automated client billing via the agency's Stripe, turning GHL into a white-label SaaS product. Breakeven at 2 clients at $297/mo.
- **Workflow Pro Plan** — Premium-actions tier ($25/$99/mo) gating inbound webhooks, AI branches, and complex conditional logic in the workflow builder.
- **AI Employee Unlimited** — $97/mo per sub-account bundle covering Conversation/Reviews/Content/Funnel AI without per-action caps. Voice AI is excluded and stays usage-based.
- **Effective monthly rate** — Annual billing converts headline rates to approximately $81 (Starter) / $248 (Unlimited) / $414 (Agency Pro) monthly — about a 16% discount vs. month-to-month.

## Relevance to AgentNexLiFy

Three strategic implications. First, AgentNexLiFy's pricing page should publish a total-cost comparator against GHL Scenario 2 and Scenario 3 — not the $297/$497 headline. The buyer-relevant comparison is `agent_os` $99.99/mo flat vs. $412/mo realistic Unlimited-agency cost, closing a ~75% price gap that today is invisible because prospects compare sticker prices. Second, AgentNexLiFy must decide whether to build SaaS-mode-equivalent reseller tooling (per-client white-label, Stripe Connect, markup sliders, snapshot deployment) or to stay direct-to-SMB. Without SaaS Mode there is no realistic path to match GHL's 10,000+ agency distribution network, but building it requires multi-quarter investment in multi-tenant billing plumbing AgentNexLiFy does not currently have. Third, the usage-fee stack is a line-item attack surface. AgentNexLiFy's flat-fee pricing is a genuine differentiator for high-volume SMBs (power-washing, plumbing, auto shops) that would hit $500+/mo in GHL usage fees alone; that buyer segment should be the first targeted cohort for outreach, not generic SMB. The anchor message is "your $297 GHL plan is $412 in reality and our $249 plan stays $249." Specific numbers beat feature bullets in SMB sales.
