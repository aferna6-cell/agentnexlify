# GoHighLevel Pricing Breakdown — SystemShift's True Monthly Cost View 2026

SystemShift's Rock Hunt published a March 2026 pricing teardown that mirrors and extends the agency-economics view already captured in [[ghl-pricing-teardown-2026]] and [[ghl-pricing-2026-true-monthly-cost-with-addons]]. The article's value is not the headline tiers — those are public — but the per-line usage rates SystemShift quotes from active GHL deployments and the displaced-tool baseline used to argue ROI to SMB buyers. For AgentNexLiFy, this is the pitch language a GHL reseller is currently using on the same plumbers, dental offices, and cleaning companies that match our ICP, and understanding the math is mandatory before competing on price or scope.

The Starter tier remains $97/mo and includes unlimited contacts, one website plus funnel builder, email marketing, two-way SMS and calling, calendar booking, pipeline, reputation, Facebook/Instagram messaging, and workflow automations. It excludes API access, white-labeling, the membership/course platform, and limits the account to three sub-accounts. Hunt budgets $30-80/mo of usage for SMS, voice, and email, putting the realistic monthly cost at $150-180. This is the first place where the article's framing diverges from naive comparisons: a published "$97" tier sells against a real "$150-180" outflow, and any competitor pricing at the same nominal number is implicitly cheaper.

Unlimited at $297/mo unlocks unlimited sub-accounts, API access, the membership and course builder, advanced reporting, and a branded desktop app, but stops short of full white-label and SaaS-mode rebilling. Usage scales with volume to $50-200/mo, putting realistic spend at $350-500. SaaS Pro at $497/mo unlocks white-label with custom domains, SaaS mode (rebill clients), and priority support; usage scales further to $100-500/mo for $600-1,000 realistic. SystemShift's numbers track within ~10% of the more aggressive teardown in [[ghl-pricing-teardown-2026]], which placed the same tiers at $107/$412/$1,591 under heavier AI Employee + A2P load — the gap is driven by whether the buyer turns Voice AI on, not by the base subscription.

The hidden-cost section is the article's most useful component for go-to-market work. Hunt cites concrete LC Phone and Twilio rates: SMS at $0.0079 per segment sent and $0.0079 received, MMS at $0.02, voice at $0.014/minute, phone numbers at $1.15/month each, and A2P 10DLC registration at $4 per campaign with a $0.003 per-message regulatory pass-through. Mailgun email gives the first 10,000/mo free then $0.0007 per additional. Premium triggers and AI-powered workflow actions carry separate per-use fees. These rates align with the carrier rate hike documented in [[ghl-carrier-pricing-april-2026]], where pass-through increases — Sierra Leone SMS +101%, Greece voice +354% — already squeezed agency margins on top of these baseline numbers. SMB buyers reading SystemShift's pitch then encountering the carrier pass-through letter from their GHL rep will see double-digit cost growth in a single month.

The $1,428/mo displaced-stack number is built from HubSpot CRM Pro ($800), Calendly Pro ($12), ClickFunnels ($147), Mailchimp Standard ($60), Twilio direct ($50), Birdeye ($300), and Teachable ($59). Even discounting HubSpot's enterprise list price as inflated, the line-item math reaches $568/mo on the cheap stack — well above Unlimited's realistic $400 — making the consolidation pitch defensible against finance-driven SMB buyers. The framing matches the broader [[ghl-ai-employee-suite-marketing-playbook]] thesis: GHL is positioned as the operating system that absorbs adjacent SaaS categories, and the pricing comparison only works because GHL is bundling into one bill what most SMBs currently pay separately. Against Salesforce Enterprise at $165/seat/month, the comparison becomes absurd by design — a 10-seat team at $1,650/mo gets CRM only, no marketing automation or telephony, which is a strawman SystemShift uses to shore up the consolidation case.

Hunt's "is it worth it" qualification is the second framing AgentNexLiFy needs to internalize. He concedes GHL is the wrong fit for buyers who need only a contact database, who run 200-person teams with enterprise compliance reporting, or who sign up and never build the workflows. The third is the most relevant: the platform without workflows is "an expensive address book." This is the activation gap our [[post-launch-growth-strategy]] has long identified — most SMBs sign up for an all-in-one platform, never build the automation, and churn in 60-90 days, which is consistent with the SMB churn baselines in [[saas-churn-benchmarks-2026]]. A widget-first product that activates lead capture and follow-up in the first session sidesteps the workflow-build dependency that even GHL's resellers admit is the actual point of failure.

The pitch construction Hunt uses is also worth lifting. He closes with "pull every invoice from your CRM, email tool, phone system, scheduler, funnel builder, and review platform," and tells the buyer that if the total exceeds $300, GHL pays for itself day one. This is a concrete, evidence-first close — match it. AgentNexLiFy's equivalent should be: "pull your missed-call log, your unanswered web inquiries, and your no-show appointment count, multiply by your average revenue per closed lead, and the widget pays for itself in the first recovered booking." The displaced-cost framing works for GHL because it absorbs SaaS line items; the recovered-revenue framing works better for our wedge because the buyer's pain is leakage, not tool cost.

## Key Concepts

- **A2P 10DLC** — Application-to-Person 10-digit Long Code messaging registration required for SMS marketing on US carriers. Carriers charge $4 per campaign registration plus a per-message fee (~$0.003) on top of base SMS rates. Compliance is mandatory or the carrier filters the traffic.
- **LC Phone** — GHL's white-labeled Twilio reseller layer. Buyers pay slightly above wholesale Twilio rates and GHL keeps the spread; this is part of why Hunt's per-segment numbers ($0.0079) are higher than Twilio's direct list price.
- **SaaS mode (rebilling)** — Agency-tier feature that lets the agency mark up GHL usage and resell to its own clients under its own brand. Required for the agency-as-platform business model and only available on $497 SaaS Pro.
- **Premium triggers** — GHL workflow actions that carry per-execution fees beyond the base subscription, including AI-powered features like Voice AI and Conversation AI. A workflow firing 10,000 times a month against a premium trigger can dwarf the base subscription in cost.
- **Realistic monthly cost** — The base subscription plus expected usage given representative volumes. Hunt's bands ($150-180 / $350-500 / $600-1,000) are the most practical lens for sizing the GHL bill and comparing to alternatives, since the published tier prices systematically understate what a working deployment actually costs.

## Relevance to AgentNexLiFy

SystemShift's article is the cleanest snapshot we have of the active GHL reseller pitch in the SMB segment we sell to. Three concrete actions follow. First, mirror the displaced-cost reasoning structure but anchor it in recovered revenue rather than displaced subscriptions — our wedge is leakage, not consolidation, and the math is more compelling because every recovered missed call clears the entire monthly cost in one booking. Second, our published price needs to undercut SystemShift's "real" $150-180 number for solo operators, not the published $97, because that is the actual benchmark a GHL-curious SMB compares us against. Third, when GHL competes head-to-head, the workflow-activation gap Hunt himself flags is the place to attack — our widget activates value in session one, where GHL requires weeks of consultant-driven workflow build before the platform pays back.
