# Ahrefs' 3M-Query "Also Rank For" Study — A #1 Page Carries ~1,000 Secondary Keywords

Ahrefs sampled 3 million random search queries and analyzed how many additional keywords the top-ranking pages also ranked for. The headline number is the load-bearing one for content strategy: the average #1 ranking page also appears in the top 10 for nearly 1,000 other relevant keywords (median ~400), and the count drops monotonically as primary-keyword position drops. The secondary count rises with primary-keyword search volume — pages ranking #1 for high-volume queries tend to harvest more secondary rankings than pages ranking #1 for low-volume queries — meaning a single well-positioned page on a popular topic compounds traffic in a way that thin pages on obscure terms cannot. The two operator-controllable factors that correlate with secondary-keyword count are content length (longer pages rank for more keywords) and URL Rating (stronger backlink profile correlates with broader keyword reach), both replicated at the #1-position cut and the top-10 cut. The study's most practical finding is that the secondary keywords overwhelmingly share common words with the primary — pages don't rank for "1,000 different topics," they rank for "1,000 query phrasings of the same topic." Ranking breadth is a function of topic depth, not topic count.

The traffic-potential reframe is the operational lesson. Ahrefs uses the keyword "I'm sorry flowers" as the worked example: monthly search volume of 250 looks unattractive in isolation, but the top-5 ranking pages each also rank for 50+ related keywords and pull ~100 highly-targeted visitors per month. The keyword research pattern that follows is two-step rather than one-step. Step one is the conventional move: pull search volume on the candidate primary keyword. Step two is the move most operators skip: pull the top-ranking pages for that keyword and inspect their organic-keywords report, counting how many secondary keywords they capture and how much aggregate traffic those secondary keywords contribute. A primary keyword with 250 searches but a competitive landscape full of pages capturing 50+ secondary keywords each is a better target than a primary keyword with 2,000 searches but a competitive landscape where the top pages capture only the primary. This reframe ports directly to vertical-SaaS content strategy: the right metric for "is this keyword worth pursuing" is the traffic potential of the cluster, not the volume of the head term.

The content-length finding replicates a pattern visible across many SEO studies: longer pages rank for more keywords on average, both at the top-10 cut and at the #1 cut. The mechanism is mechanical — longer content covers more sub-questions, more semantically-related phrasings, more entity mentions — but the operator's takeaway is not "write longer to rank more." The takeaway is that the page must actually answer the breadth of questions a user of that topic might phrase, and breadth-answering correlates with length. Padding a 600-word post to 2,400 words with restated paragraphs does not produce the same lift as a 2,400-word post that genuinely walks the entity graph. The keyword-cluster planning practice that follows from this finding is to pre-enumerate the secondary phrasings before writing — pull the top-ranking pages, list every distinct sub-question they cover, and ensure the new page covers the same set with the same or better depth. Length comes for free when the breadth is real.

The URL Rating correlation surfaces the second operator lever: backlinks. Ahrefs' URL Rating is a per-URL backlink-strength score, and the study shows a strong positive correlation between URL Rating and number of "also rank for" keywords, both at top-10 and at #1. The mechanism here is competitive — for high-volume head terms, ranking is a function of authority signals, and authority transfers across the secondary keywords too. The operator implication for AgentNexLiFy is that a vertical landing page launched without a backlink-acquisition plan will harvest a fraction of the secondary keywords it would harvest with one. The cost-effective backlink-acquisition strategies for vertical SaaS — guest posts on industry blogs, listings in vertical directories, partnership cross-links with adjacent (non-competitive) vendors, and native PR placements — should run parallel to content production, not after. A landing page hitting publish without a 5-link backlink target is a page that will rank for the primary keyword (if it ranks at all) but miss the 50-100x secondary-keyword multiplier.

The "topic depth, not topic stuffing" finding is the methodological correction to a common mistake. Operators sometimes try to maximize keyword coverage by writing pages that span multiple semi-related topics — a "guide to plumbing AND HVAC AND electrical" page, on the theory that more topics equals more keywords. Ahrefs' data shows this fails: the secondary keywords on a high-ranking page are overwhelmingly variations of the primary keyword's topic, not separate topics. Ranking for "1,000 different topics with one page" is essentially nonexistent in the dataset; ranking for "1,000 phrasings of the same topic" is the dominant pattern. The right page architecture is one focused topic, deeply addressed, with secondary keywords being the natural variations a user might phrase the same question — not a topic-cluster mashup. For AgentNexLiFy's vertical pages, this means a "salon-AI-receptionist" page should go deep on every salon-specific phrasing, scenario, and feature interaction — not bolt on a "salon and dental and chiropractor" attempt to triple-dip categories. Vertical-SaaS landing pages are stronger as narrow-and-deep instances than as broad-and-shallow umbrellas.

The operational architecture for AgentNexLiFy's vertical-page program follows from the four findings. First, candidate-keyword evaluation runs the two-step traffic-potential check, not the volume-only check — pull the top-ranking pages for each candidate primary, count secondary keywords, prefer cluster traffic potential over head volume. Second, page production targets one vertical per page, deeply enumerated — every salon-specific phrasing of "AI receptionist," every plumber-specific scenario, every contractor-specific feature interaction, with the secondary-keyword cluster pre-enumerated from competitor analysis. Third, content length is allowed to scale with breadth — if the breadth-answer requires 3,000 words, write 3,000; if it requires 800, write 800; do not pad. Fourth, every published page enters a backlink-acquisition queue with a target of 5+ industry-relevant links within 90 days, because the URL Rating correlation says the secondary-keyword multiplier is locked behind authority signals. The compound result of all four moves is the difference between a vertical page that captures 1,000 visitors/month and one that captures 50.

## Key Concepts

- **"Also rank for" keywords** — Secondary keywords a top-ranking page captures beyond its primary head keyword; the average #1 page in Ahrefs' 3M-query sample captures ~1,000 secondary top-10 rankings (median ~400).
- **Traffic potential** — The aggregate organic-traffic value of a keyword cluster (primary + secondary), measured by inspecting top-ranking pages' organic-keywords reports rather than the head keyword's search volume in isolation.
- **URL Rating correlation** — Ahrefs' per-URL backlink-strength score correlates strongly with secondary-keyword count both at the top-10 and #1 cuts, meaning authority transfers across the cluster, not just to the head term.
- **Content length effect** — Longer pages rank for more keywords on average; the mechanism is breadth-answer (more sub-questions covered, more entity mentions) rather than length itself, so padding does not replicate the effect.
- **Topic depth vs topic stuffing** — Secondary keywords on a ranking page are overwhelmingly variations of the primary topic, not separate topics; multi-topic mashup pages do not capture additive secondary-keyword counts.
- **Long-tail compounding** — Ranking for thousands of long-tail keywords with a single well-positioned page is normal; ranking for multiple high-volume (10k+) keywords with one page is very rare and not a realistic target.

## Relevance to AgentNexLiFy

The 3M-query study reframes vertical-page strategy from "head-keyword chase" to "cluster-traffic capture." For AgentNexLiFy's planned vertical landing pages (salon, plumbing, dental, contractor, legal, real estate, restaurant, auto-shop, medical-office), the keyword-research workflow is two-step: pull search volume on the candidate primary keyword, then pull the top-ranking pages and count their secondary-keyword harvest via Ahrefs' or SemRush's organic-keywords report. Pages targeting "AI receptionist for plumbers" with 200 monthly searches but a competitive landscape capturing 50+ secondary keywords each are higher-value targets than "AI chatbot software" with 5,000 searches but where top pages capture only the head term. The page production architecture is one vertical per page, deeply enumerated — every plumber-specific phrasing, scenario, feature interaction, and competitor comparison the user might search — with a pre-enumerated secondary-keyword cluster pulled from competitor analysis before writing begins.

The two operator-controllable levers — content length and URL Rating — both require investment beyond the page itself. Content length scales naturally with breadth-of-answer; the rule is not "write 3,000 words" but "answer every sub-question a vertical-specific user might phrase," which lands at 1,500-3,500 words for most vertical pages. URL Rating requires a parallel backlink-acquisition queue: 5+ industry-relevant links per page within 90 days of publish, sourced from vertical directories (e.g. plumber association websites, salon industry blogs), guest-post placements on adjacent-vertical SaaS blogs (CRMs, scheduling tools, accounting software for the same vertical), and partnership cross-links. Without the backlink layer, the secondary-keyword multiplier is locked and the page captures a fraction of available cluster traffic. The competitive read is that GoHighLevel, Smith.ai, and the larger AI-receptionist platforms have established URL Rating across thousands of vertical landing pages; AgentNexLiFy's vertical-SEO program is a backlinks-and-depth game played at scale, not a publish-and-pray game played one page at a time.
