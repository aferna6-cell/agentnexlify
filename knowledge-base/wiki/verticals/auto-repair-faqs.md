Auto repair is a strong vertical for AgentNexLiFy because a car problem creates urgency and the visitor needs answers before they commit: my check engine light is on, how much is a brake job, can you look at it today, do you give estimates, will you tow it. A driver with a noise or a warning light usually calls or messages two or three shops, and the one that answers fast and gets the car scheduled wins the job. The questions below are the ones drivers actually type into a chat widget on an auto shop site. The answers are written generic so they apply across any auto repair tenant, and a specific shop overrides the ranges, hours, and policies with its own values during onboarding. The content here is what the full-text search indexes, so each question is phrased the way a customer phrases it and each answer is short enough to send as a chat reply.

Auto repair pricing depends on the vehicle, the parts, and what the actual problem turns out to be, so the answers use ranges and "needs a diagnostic to confirm" language on purpose. A brake job on a small sedan costs less than one on a large truck, and a check engine light can be a cheap sensor or a serious repair, which means a fixed quote over chat would be wrong for most drivers. The honest move is to give a typical range, explain that a diagnostic confirms the real number, and book the car in. Urgency handling matters more here than for a salon: drivers with a car that will not start, a towing need, or a safety issue like brakes get a fast path to scheduling, and the answers cover towing, loaners, and how long common jobs take.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My check engine light is on. What does that mean?**
A check engine light can mean many things, from a loose gas cap to a sensor or an engine issue, and the only way to know is a diagnostic scan that reads the trouble code. If the light is flashing or the car is running rough, it is safer to bring it in soon. We can book a diagnostic and tell you the typical cost to run it.

**How much will the repair cost?**
The cost depends on your vehicle, the parts it needs, and what the actual problem is, so a real number comes after a diagnostic or inspection. We can give a typical range for common jobs, and once we see the car we confirm the price before doing any work. Tell us the vehicle and the symptom and we can get closer.

**Do you give free estimates?**
Many shops give a free estimate for visible work like brakes or a quote after a quick look, while a full diagnostic to find an unknown problem may have a fee that often goes toward the repair if you proceed. Policies vary by shop. Tell us the issue and we will explain how the estimate works here.

**How much is an oil change?**
Oil change price depends on your vehicle and the oil type, with conventional on the lower end and full synthetic higher. Many shops include a basic check of fluids and tires with it. We can give a range for your vehicle and book a time, since most oil changes are quick.

**How much is a brake job?**
Brake cost depends on the vehicle and whether you need pads only or pads and rotors, and front and rear can differ. A small car is on the lower end and a large truck or SUV is higher. If you hear grinding or feel a pulse when braking, get it checked soon. We can give a range and book an inspection.

**Can you look at my car today or this week?**
Same-day and same-week openings depend on the shop's schedule and how urgent the issue is. A car that will not start or a safety problem gets priority over routine maintenance. Tell us the vehicle and the issue and we can show you the soonest opening or a drop-off time.

**What are your hours?**
Most auto shops are open weekdays during business hours, with some open on Saturday and fewer on Sunday. Many take their last drop-off for same-day work earlier in the afternoon so they can finish before close. Check the specific shop for exact hours, since they vary by day.

**Do you offer towing or can I have it towed to you?**
Many shops can arrange a tow or work with a towing company, and you can also have your car towed to them directly. If your car is not drivable, tell us where it is and we can explain the towing options and get you scheduled for when it arrives.

**Do you give a loaner car or a ride while my car is being fixed?**
Some shops offer loaner cars, a shuttle, or a ride service for longer repairs, while others do not. It depends on the shop and the length of the job. Ask us and we can tell you what is available and arrange it when you book.

**How long will the repair take?**
Time depends on the job. An oil change or a simple service is often under an hour, brakes can be a couple of hours, and bigger repairs like a major engine or transmission job can take a day or more, partly depending on parts. We give a time estimate once we know the work.

**Do you work on my make and model?**
Most general repair shops service common makes and models, and some specialize in certain brands, diesels, or hybrids and electrics. Tell us your year, make, and model and we can confirm the shop works on it before you come in.

**Do you do inspections, smog, or emissions testing?**
Many shops do safety inspections and some are licensed for smog or emissions testing where it is required, but not all are. It depends on the shop and your area's rules. Tell us what you need and we can confirm whether the shop handles it.

**Is there a warranty on the work and parts?**
Many shops offer a warranty on parts and labor, with the length depending on the shop and the part. New parts often carry their own manufacturer warranty too. Ask us and we can tell you the warranty terms for the repair you need.

**Can you do scheduled or factory maintenance?**
Most shops handle scheduled maintenance like oil, filters, fluids, and the service intervals in your owner's manual, and this often costs less than dealer pricing while keeping your records in order. Tell us your mileage and we can recommend what is due and book it.

**Do I need an appointment or can I drop off my car?**
Many shops take both appointments and drop-offs, and an appointment usually means a faster turnaround. For an unknown problem, dropping off lets the shop diagnose it when a tech is free. Tell us your situation and we can set up an appointment or a drop-off time.

**What forms of payment do you take?**
Most auto shops take major cards and cash, and many take digital payments. Some offer financing for larger repairs. Check the specific shop for its accepted methods, and we can confirm when you schedule.

## Key Concepts

- **Diagnostic-first pricing**: A real price comes after a scan or inspection, so the AI front desk gives a typical range and books the car in rather than quoting a final number over chat.
- **Urgency triage**: A non-starting car, towing need, or brake safety issue gets a faster scheduling path than routine maintenance.
- **Towing and loaners**: Drivers with an undrivable car ask about tow arrangements and loaner or shuttle options, which are tenant settings the AI front desk can surface.
- **Vehicle fit check**: Year, make, and model determine whether the shop services the car, so the AI front desk confirms fit before booking.
- **Estimate policy**: Free visible-work estimates versus a diagnostic fee that may apply to the repair is a tenant setting the AI front desk explains.
- **Warranty terms**: Many shops warranty parts and labor, and drivers ask before committing.
