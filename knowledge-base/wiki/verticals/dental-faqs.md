## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are you accepting new patients?**
Many dental practices welcome new patients and can usually schedule a first visit within a week or two. Tell us a little about what you need, a cleaning, an exam, or a specific concern, and we can get you on the schedule. New patients often start with an exam and cleaning.

**Do you take my insurance?**
Practices work with a range of dental plans, and whether yours is in network depends on the specific carrier and policy. Share your insurance name and we will pass it to the team to confirm coverage, since checking a specific policy is handled by staff. Many offices also see out-of-network patients and can file the claim for you.

**What if I do not have insurance?**
You can still be seen without insurance. Many practices offer self-pay pricing, and some have membership or discount plans for routine care. Tell us what you need and we can share the options and book your visit.

**How much is a cleaning or a checkup?**
The cost of a routine cleaning and exam depends on your insurance and whether x-rays are needed. With insurance, preventive visits are often covered or low cost. Without insurance, a cleaning and exam falls into a self-pay range that the office can confirm. Ask and we can give a closer idea.

**How much does a filling, crown, or other treatment cost?**
Treatment pricing varies by the procedure, the materials, and your coverage. A filling is at the lower end and a crown or root canal is higher. The dentist confirms what you need at the exam and the office gives you a cost estimate before any work. We can book the exam to get you that number.

**What are your hours?**
Most dental offices are open on weekdays, with some offering early-morning, evening, or Saturday appointments. Hours vary by location and by provider. Check the specific office for exact hours and which days each provider is in.

**Can I be seen today? I have a toothache.**
Severe tooth pain is treated as urgent by most offices, and many keep room for same-day emergency visits. Tell us your symptoms and we will get you to the soonest available appointment. If it is after hours, we will route you to the right next step.

**I knocked out or broke a tooth. What do I do?**
A knocked-out or broken tooth is a dental emergency, so time matters. If a tooth came out, keep it moist, handle it by the crown not the root, and seek care quickly. Let us get you in as soon as possible and we will note the details for the dentist.

**My face or gums are swollen. Should I come in?**
Swelling can signal an infection that needs prompt attention. Let us get you a same-day or urgent appointment, and if the swelling is severe or affects your breathing or swallowing, treat it as a medical emergency and seek immediate care.

**How often should I get a cleaning?**
Most patients do well with a cleaning and checkup every six months, though some need more frequent visits based on their oral health. The dentist recommends the right schedule for you at your exam. We can set up your next cleaning now.

**Do you see children, and at what age should they start?**
Many practices see children, and some are family or general offices that treat all ages. Guidance commonly points to a first dental visit by around age one or when the first tooth appears. Ask about the location and we can confirm whether they treat kids and book the visit.

**Do you offer teeth whitening or cosmetic services?**
Many practices offer whitening and cosmetic options like veneers or bonding. Availability and pricing depend on the office and on what suits your teeth. Tell us what you are interested in and we can book a consultation.

**Do you do braces, Invisalign, or implants?**
Some general dentists offer orthodontics like clear aligners or implants, while others refer you to a specialist. It depends on the practice. Tell us what you are looking for and we can confirm whether the office provides it or arranges a referral.

**What happens at a first visit?**
A first visit usually includes a health and dental history, an exam, x-rays if needed, and often a cleaning. Plan to arrive a few minutes early to complete new-patient forms, or fill them out ahead if the office sends them. The dentist reviews findings and any recommended care with you.

**Do I need to fill out forms before I come in?**
New patients typically complete a health history and contact form. Many offices send these ahead so your first visit moves faster. We can collect your basic details now and the office will handle the rest securely.

**Do you offer payment plans or financing?**
Many practices offer payment plans or third-party financing for larger treatment like crowns, implants, or orthodontics. Terms depend on the provider. We can note your interest and the team will go over the options at your visit.

**I am nervous about the dentist. Can you help?**
Dental anxiety is common, and many offices are used to helping nervous patients feel comfortable, with options like clear explanations, breaks during treatment, or sedation in some practices. Let us know your concerns and we will share them with the team so your visit goes smoothly.

**What payment methods do you accept?**
Most dental offices take cards and many take digital payments, plus cash or check, and they file insurance claims for covered care. Larger treatment may have payment-plan options. Check the specific office for its accepted methods.

**Can I move or cancel my appointment?**
Yes. Most offices ask for notice before your appointment, often 24 to 48 hours, so the time can go to another patient. Tell us your appointment and the new time you want and we can reschedule it.

## Key Concepts

- **Insurance first**: Coverage is usually the patient's first question. The AI front desk stays general and routes a specific policy check to staff, since it touches personal health information.
- **HIPAA-aware answers**: The front desk gives general guidance and captures basic details, while specifics about a person's record or coverage go to the office. This mirrors the platform's HIPAA-aware behavior.
- **Self-pay and membership pricing**: Uninsured patients get a self-pay range or a membership-plan option rather than a flat quote.
- **Dental emergency triage**: Severe pain, swelling, a knocked-out tooth, and a broken tooth route to same-day or urgent care, with a note to seek medical help when swelling threatens breathing.
- **New-patient intake**: First visits include history, exam, x-rays, and forms. The platform supports a patient intake form preset for this.
- **Recall schedule**: A six-month cleaning cadence is the common default, with rebook automation closing the loop.
