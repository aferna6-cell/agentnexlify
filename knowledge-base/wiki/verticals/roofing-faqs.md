Roofing and general-contractor work is a high-ticket trade where the deciding question is rarely "what is it" and almost always "what will it cost and can you start soon," so the FAQ content leads with free inspections, the repair-versus-replacement split, and storm-damage insurance handling. A homeowner with a leak after a storm is shopping three contractors at once, and the one whose site answers the inspection and claims questions first books the appointment. These answers are written generic so they apply across any roofing or contractor tenant, with specific service areas, fees, material lines, and warranty terms set during onboarding. The content here is what full-text search indexes, so each question is phrased the way a homeowner phrases it after a storm or when a roof is near end of life.

Pricing answers use ranges and "depends on the roof" language because square footage, pitch, material, layers to tear off, and access all move the number, and a roofer quotes after seeing the roof. The emergency answers cover active leaks and storm damage, where a temporary tarp and a fast inspection matter more than a full quote on day one. The insurance answers stay general: the contractor inspects, documents damage, and works with the adjuster, but the homeowner files the claim and the carrier decides coverage. That split keeps the AI front desk helpful without making promises about what insurance will pay.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do you offer free inspections or estimates?**
Many roofing and contractor companies give a free inspection and written estimate for larger jobs like a roof replacement, a re-roof, or major repairs. Smaller diagnostic visits sometimes carry a fee. Tell us your address and what you are seeing and we can book an inspection and confirm whether it is free for your job.

**My roof is leaking right now. Can someone come out?**
An active leak is urgent because water gets into the structure fast. If it is safe, put a bucket under the drip and move valuables clear. Send us your address and what is happening and we will get you the soonest available visit. Many companies can place a temporary tarp to stop the water before the full repair.

**Do you do emergency or storm-damage repairs?**
Many companies handle emergency leak repair and storm damage from wind, hail, or fallen limbs. After a storm the schedule fills quickly, so tell us your address and the damage you see and we will get you in line for an inspection and any temporary protection your roof needs.

**Do I need a repair or a full replacement?**
That depends on the roof's age, the size of the damage, and how many layers are already up there. A small isolated leak is often a repair, while widespread wear, a roof near the end of its life, or storm damage across the surface points toward replacement. An inspector tells you which makes sense after seeing it, and many will explain both options with pricing.

**How much does a new roof or a repair cost?**
Pricing depends on the size and pitch of the roof, the material you choose, how many old layers come off, and access. Repairs are usually a smaller flat range, and full replacements get a written estimate after an inspection. Tell us the rough size and what is going on and we can give a ballpark, then book an inspection for a real number.

**Will you work with my insurance on a storm-damage claim?**
Many roofing companies inspect the damage, document it with photos, and meet your adjuster on site to walk the claim. You file the claim with your carrier, and the carrier decides what is covered. We can schedule an inspection so you have documentation before or during the claim. We cannot promise what your insurance will pay, since that is the carrier's call.

**What roofing materials or options do you offer?**
Companies commonly install asphalt shingle, metal, tile, and flat or low-slope systems, and many offer a few quality tiers within each. The right choice depends on your home, your budget, and your local climate. Tell us what you have now and what you are after, and we can go over the options at the inspection.

**Do you offer a warranty or guarantee?**
Most companies back their labor with a workmanship warranty, and the roofing materials usually carry a separate manufacturer warranty. Lengths and terms vary by company and product. We can confirm the warranty that applies to your repair or replacement once we know the job.

**How long does a roof replacement take?**
Many residential roofs are torn off and replaced in one to three days depending on size, pitch, material, and weather. Larger or more complex roofs take longer, and rain can push the schedule. We give a realistic timeline once an inspector sees the roof.

**Are you licensed, bonded, and insured?**
Reputable roofing and contractor companies carry the license, bond, and insurance their state and trade require. The specifics depend on your area. We can confirm the credentials for the company handling your job so you have them before work starts.

**Do you offer financing on a roof or large project?**
Many companies offer financing for big-ticket work like a full replacement or a major remodel. Approval and terms depend on the provider. Tell us you are interested and the team can walk you through the financing options when you get your estimate.

**What areas do you serve?**
Companies usually cover a set of towns or a radius around their base. Tell us your city or zip code and we can confirm you are in the service area and book an inspection. If you are just outside it, we will let you know any travel consideration.

**How soon can you start the work?**
Start timing depends on the schedule, the size of the job, material availability, and the weather. Emergency leak protection is fast, while a full replacement is scheduled out once the estimate is approved and materials are ordered. We can give you a realistic start window after the inspection.

**Do you do gutters, siding, or other exterior work?**
Many roofing companies also handle gutters, siding, fascia, soffit, and related exterior repairs, and general contractors take a wider range of projects. Tell us everything you need and we can confirm what this company covers and book one visit for it.

**Will you handle the permits and cleanup?**
Most companies pull the permits a job requires and clean up the site, including running a magnet for nails when roofing work is done. The details vary by job and by area. We can confirm what is included in your estimate.

**How do I get started?**
The first step is an inspection so a pro can see the roof or project and give you a real number. Send us your address, what is going on, and a good time, and we will book the visit. If you have photos of the damage, those help the inspector come prepared.

## Key Concepts

- **Free inspection and estimate**: The entry point for most roofing leads. Larger jobs get a free inspection and written estimate, while small diagnostic visits may carry a fee, and the split is a tenant setting.
- **Repair versus replacement**: The core decision driven by roof age, damage spread, and existing layers. Answers explain both paths and defer the call to an on-site inspector.
- **Storm-damage and leak emergency**: Active leaks and post-storm damage are flagged urgent and routed to a fast inspection plus temporary tarp protection.
- **Insurance claim support, not promises**: The contractor inspects and documents and meets the adjuster, but the homeowner files and the carrier decides coverage. Answers stay general on what insurance pays.
- **Material tiers**: Asphalt, metal, tile, and flat systems with quality tiers; the choice is a per-tenant and per-home conversation at the inspection.
- **Workmanship versus manufacturer warranty**: Two separate warranties on every job, labor from the company and product from the maker, with terms set per tenant.
