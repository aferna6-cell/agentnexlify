Veterinary clinics carry two traits that shape their FAQ content: pet owners worry first about whether a problem is an emergency, and the clinic practices medicine, so the AI front desk books appointments and answers logistics but never diagnoses. The questions below are the ones owners type into a clinic site chat: are you taking new patients, can I bring my dog in today, is this an emergency, how much is a checkup, do you take walk-ins. An AI front desk that answers these and captures the new-patient booking fills the schedule before the owner calls the clinic across town. Every medical or emergency question routes the owner to call the clinic directly or reach an emergency vet now, because a chat widget is not the place to assess a sick animal. These answers are generic so they apply across any veterinary tenant, with specific hours, services, and fees set during onboarding.

Pricing answers use ranges and "depends on your pet" language because species, size, age, and the exam findings move the cost, and the vet quotes after seeing the animal. The emergency answers are the highest-stakes part of this pack: any sign of a real emergency, trouble breathing, a suspected poisoning, a serious injury, collapse, or a bloated hard belly, gets one clear instruction to call the clinic now or go to the nearest emergency vet, with no attempt to judge severity over chat. That hard routing keeps the widget useful for booking and logistics while keeping medical judgment with licensed staff.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this an emergency? My pet seems really sick or hurt.**
If your pet is in trouble, please call the clinic right now or go to the nearest emergency vet. Signs that need immediate care include trouble breathing, collapse, a seizure, a suspected poisoning, heavy bleeding, a serious injury, or a hard swollen belly. We cannot assess your pet over chat, so the safest step is to call the clinic or an emergency animal hospital straight away.

**Do you handle emergencies or after-hours care?**
Some clinics see urgent cases during business hours and others refer after-hours emergencies to a dedicated emergency animal hospital. If your pet needs help right now, call the clinic or the nearest emergency vet rather than waiting. We can tell you how this clinic handles urgent cases and after-hours coverage, and book a regular visit for non-urgent concerns.

**My pet ate something they shouldn't have. What do I do?**
Please treat this as urgent and call the clinic now, or contact an emergency vet or a pet poison helpline right away, since timing matters with anything swallowed. We cannot tell you whether it is dangerous over chat. Reaching a person who can advise on your specific pet is the safe move.

**How do I book an appointment?**
You can book right here in the chat, on the website booking page, or by phone during business hours. To book we confirm your pet's name and species, the reason for the visit, and an open time. New pets may need a few extra details. You get a confirmation by text or email.

**Are you accepting new patients?**
Many clinics welcome new patients and will set up a first visit so the vet can meet your pet and start a record. Availability depends on the clinic and the schedule. Tell us a bit about your pet and we can check openings and get the first appointment booked.

**Do you take walk-ins?**
Some clinics take walk-ins when there is room and others see pets by appointment only, often keeping a few slots open for same-day needs. For anything urgent, call the clinic first so they can prepare. Tell us what your pet needs and we can check whether a walk-in works or book the soonest appointment.

**What services do you offer?**
Clinics commonly offer wellness exams, vaccines, spay and neuter, dental cleanings, diagnostics like bloodwork and x-rays, and surgery. Many also handle microchipping, parasite prevention, and senior-pet care. Tell us what your pet needs and we can confirm the clinic offers it and book the right visit.

**Do you do vaccines and wellness checkups?**
Yes, routine wellness exams and vaccines are a core part of most clinics. These keep your pet healthy and catch problems early, and the vet recommends the right schedule for your pet's age and lifestyle. We can book a wellness visit and the front desk will confirm which vaccines are due.

**Do you perform surgery, spay, neuter, or dental work?**
Many clinics handle spay and neuter, dental cleanings, and other surgical procedures. These usually start with an exam so the vet can plan the procedure and explain the cost for your specific pet. We can book that first exam and the team will walk you through the next steps.

**How much does a visit or service cost?**
Cost depends on your pet's species, size, and age, and on what the vet finds during the exam. A routine wellness visit falls in a range, while vaccines, dental work, and surgery are quoted based on your pet. The vet gives the specifics after seeing your pet. Tell us the reason for the visit and we can give a rough idea and book the appointment.

**What do I need for a first visit?**
For a first visit, bring any records you have, including past vaccines and medical history, and a list of any medications or food your pet is on. Note any symptoms or changes you have seen. Arriving a few minutes early helps the team set up your pet's record. We can collect some details when you book.

**Do you take pet insurance?**
Pet insurance usually works as reimbursement: you pay the clinic and your insurer pays you back based on your plan. Most clinics give you the paperwork you need to file a claim. Coverage and reimbursement depend on your specific policy, so check with your insurer on what is included. We can note that you have insurance on your record.

**What forms of payment do you take?**
Most clinics take cards and many take digital payments, plus cash, and some offer pet-care financing or payment plans for larger procedures. Payment is usually due at the time of service. Check the specific clinic for its accepted methods and any financing options.

**What are your hours?**
Clinics keep set daytime hours, often with some evening or weekend availability, and many refer after-hours emergencies to an emergency animal hospital. Check the specific clinic for exact hours and how it handles urgent cases outside those hours.

**Can I refill my pet's medication or food?**
Many clinics handle prescription refills and special diets, though some refills need a recent exam first. Tell us your pet's name and what you need and we can pass the refill request to the team, who will confirm whether an exam is required before they can fill it.

**Can I reschedule or cancel my appointment?**
Yes. Tell us your pet's name and the appointment and we can move it to another open time or cancel it. Many clinics ask for notice so the slot can go to another pet. We will confirm the new time by text or email.

## Key Concepts

- **Emergency routing, not diagnosis**: Any medical or emergency question gets one instruction, call the clinic now or reach an emergency vet, with no severity judgment over chat. This is the hard safety rule for the vertical.
- **New-patient booking**: The highest-value conversion. The AI front desk captures species, reason, and contact, and books the first exam so the vet can start a record.
- **Wellness versus procedure**: Routine exams and vaccines book directly, while surgery, spay, neuter, and dental usually start with an exam so the vet can plan and price the procedure.
- **Walk-in versus appointment**: Clinic-specific. The answer steers urgent cases to call first and books the soonest slot for the rest.
- **Pet insurance as reimbursement**: Owners pay the clinic and file with their insurer for reimbursement, so answers stay general and route coverage questions back to the policy.
- **Pricing by pet**: Species, size, age, and exam findings drive cost, so answers use ranges and defer specifics to the vet after the exam.
